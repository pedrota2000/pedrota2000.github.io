About me

I am a PhD student in theoretical physics at the Institute of Cosmos Sciences (ICCUB), Universitat de Barcelona, supervised by David Mateos and Raúl Jiménez and funded by an FPI fellowship (Ministerio de Ciencia e Innovación) as part of the project Dark Energy and the Origin of the Universe. I expect to defend my thesis in 2027 and am looking for postdoctoral positions starting in autumn 2027.

I work on strongly coupled matter and gravity, and on what they tell us about cosmology and astrophysics. Much of it revolves around first-order phase transitions, in the early universe or inside neutron star mergers. These proceed through bubbles that nucleate, expand and collide, and the fluid motion they stir up is a source of gravitational waves. I study how these bubbles expand when the equation of state is realistic rather than conformal, and I use holography (AdS/CFT) to describe the strongly coupled, far-from-equilibrium dynamics that sits underneath.

Holography also turns many physical questions into inverse problems. One example is finding the gravitational dual of a given gauge theory using only its equation of state. Problems like this are stiff and highly non-linear, and I tackle them with physics-informed neural networks (PINNs). That work has fed back into machine learning itself: new regularization schemes that let PINNs transfer-learn into stiff regimes, and the question of what a network actually learns when it solves a whole family of differential equations at once.

I am a member of the Simons Collaboration for the Geometry of Flows, where I work on turbulence, and I have contributed to Denario, a multi-agent AI system for scientific research. Most of my work is collaborative. A research stay with Pavlos Protopapas at Harvard SEAS in 2024 started a series of joint papers, and in December 2026 I will visit the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton to work on turbulence with James Stone.

Before my PhD I completed an MSc in Theoretical Physics at the Instituto de Física Teórica (IFT), UAM-CSIC — where my thesis on Quantum Chaos in de Sitter Space and Centaur Geometries was supervised by Juan F. Pedraza and Ayan Kumar Patra — and a BSc in Physics at the Universidad Complutense de Madrid.

Research interests

🔭

Holographic Inverse Problems

Reconstructing the gravitational dual of a strongly coupled gauge theory directly from its equation of state, by solving the bulk Einstein–scalar equations as an inverse problem with PINNs. The method now reaches theories with large hierarchies of scales, false vacua and skipping renormalization-group flows.

🌌

Phase Transitions & Gravitational Waves

Bubble expansion in first-order phase transitions beyond the conformal approximation. Non-conformal equations of state obstruct some expansion modes, open new ones (“shocked detonations”) and, for QCD-like theories, rule out detonations entirely. This changes the energy available for gravitational waves from the early universe and neutron star mergers.

🌊

Turbulence & Geometry of Flows

Turbulence and hydrodynamic instabilities, from holography, where the flow is encoded in the geometry of the bulk, to vortex dynamics. Member of the Simons Collaboration for the Geometry of Flows, and contributor of turbulence forcing to the AthenaK code.

🧠

Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Multi-head PINNs in which a shared body learns a whole family of solutions, with a unimodular regularization of the latent space that makes transfer learning into stiff regimes efficient. Applied to holography and to other inverse problems, such as the Calderón problem of electrical impedance tomography.

📐

What Networks Learn about PDEs

Embedding families of PDE solutions (Burgers, heat, wave) in a learned latent manifold. A handful of principal components carry almost all of the variance, and how they spread over Fourier shells is reproducible across training runs. It is a property of the solution space, not of a particular training.

🤖

AI Agents for Science

Contributing to Denario, a modular multi-agent system that generates research ideas, reviews the literature, writes and runs code, and drafts papers across scientific disciplines.

Non-conformal obstructions to bubble expansion
D. Mateos, M. Sanchez-Garitaonandia, P. Tarancón-Álvarez
arXiv:2607.27874 [hep-th]

Self-similar bubble solutions for equations of state ranging from the bag model to QCD-like theories, with pure SU(3) Yang–Mills as a benchmark. Non-conformality creates new obstructions to expansion and a new branch of “shocked detonations”. For QCD-like theories every detonation is excluded, which lowers the kinetic energy available for gravitational waves.

Physics-Informed Neural Embeddings of PDE Solution Families
R. Jimenez, S. Mayboroda, P. Protopapas, L. Sarieddine, D. N. Spergel, P. Tarancón-Álvarez
arXiv:2607.06348

A multi-head PINN learns a latent manifold for a whole family of PDE solutions. For Burgers dynamics, two to four principal components carry about 95% of the latent variance. How they spread over Fourier shells is the same in independent training runs, so it gives an observable of the solution space itself. Heat and wave equations serve as checks.
